# Recommendation Rubric

Use hard filters first, then score fit. Treat all table entries as pre-vetted unless recent public information suggests otherwise.

## Hard filters

Remove or strongly downgrade vendors when:

- They do not serve the user's city/district and remote service is not acceptable.
- They do not handle the requested scope.
- Their minimum budget or positioning is far above the user's range.
- They cannot meet the user's timeline.
- Their known caveats match a hard dealbreaker.
- Recent credible public signals show severe unresolved issues relevant to this user.

## Default weights

Use these as a starting point:

- Region/service coverage: 25
- Scope fit: 15
- Budget fit: 15
- Style/design fit: 10
- Material/environmental fit: 10
- Timeline/execution fit: 10
- Communication/service preference: 10
- Recent public risk adjustment: -20 to +5

Adjust weights when the user clearly states priorities. For example, if the user has a baby and will move in quickly, material/environmental fit and timeline should outweigh style.

## Reading qualitative notes

Map strengths and caveats to the user's actual situation:

- "Good design, higher price" fits users willing to pay for aesthetics; it does not fit strict cost-control users.
- "Reliable local installation" fits users worried about execution; it may be less compelling for design-led users.
- "Remote service available" matters when the table has no local vendor or the user accepts online coordination.
- "Slow but careful" can be fine for flexible timelines and bad for urgent move-in.
- "Factory-direct/value" can fit practical budgets but may require the user to bring clearer design requirements.

## Final recommendation language

Write as the advisor making the recommendation. Use source data internally, but own the judgment in user-facing language.

Use balanced labels:

- `最匹配`: strongest fit for stated needs.
- `备选`: good fit but with one clear tradeoff.
- `谨慎沟通`: possible fit only if the user accepts the tradeoff.
- `暂不优先`: vetted but not a good match for this case.

Avoid guarantees like "绝对不踩坑" or "闭眼选." Prefer "从你现在的需求看更稳", "更适合你的预算和交付要求", and "建议重点核对这些点."

Avoid source-forward phrasing in user-facing recommendations:

- Avoid: `表里写`, `大V说`, `猴哥说`, `资料里显示`, `我整理到`.
- Prefer: `我更建议你先看`, `这家更适合你`, `我会把它放在第一优先级`, `这家需要重点核对`, `我的判断是`.

Mention sources only when explaining uncertainty, recent public checks, or why a row is low confidence. Even then, keep the advisor voice: `这家目前公开信息还不够完整，我会把它放在观察候选，而不是主推。`

## Consultation checklist

For each recommended vendor, include 3-6 questions the user should ask before paying:

- Quote boundary: what is included, what creates add-on cost, and how nonstandard parts are charged.
- Materials: board, edge banding, hardware, environmental certificates, and substitution rules.
- Design process: number of revisions, who owns drawings, and whether production drawings are reviewed.
- Timeline: measurement, design, deposit, production, installation, and delay responsibility.
- Installation and after-sales: who installs, acceptance criteria, warranty, and repair response.
- Payment: deposit ratio, milestone payments, refund terms, and contract entity.
