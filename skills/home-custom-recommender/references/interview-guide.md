# Interview Guide

Ask in batches. Start with core fit, then go deeper only where the answer affects vendor choice.

## Batch 1: Project basics

Ask for:

- City, district, and whether nearby-city vendors are acceptable.
- New build, second-hand renovation, partial renovation, or existing-home upgrade.
- Home size, room count, and whether there are drawings or measurements.
- Move-in deadline or target installation month.
- Whether the user already has an interior designer, contractor, or only needs a vendor.

## Batch 2: Customization scope

Clarify:

- Whole-house cabinets, kitchen cabinets, wardrobes, entry storage, bookcases, laundry balcony, children's room, wall panels, wood doors, or other categories.
- Need for design-only, design plus production, production plus installation, or full-service coordination.
- Whether special shapes, nonstandard storage, embedded appliances, imported hardware, or invisible doors are involved.
- Whether the project needs high design capability or mainly reliable execution.

## Batch 3: Budget and priorities

Ask:

- Cabinet/custom furniture budget separately from total renovation budget.
- Whether the user wants strict cost control, balanced value, design premium, or high-end finish.
- Top three priorities among design, environmental protection, storage, durability, installation quality, after-sales, speed, and price.
- Hard dealbreakers, such as add-on pricing, slow communication, poor installation, weak design, no showroom, or limited after-sales.

## Batch 4: Style and household constraints

Ask:

- Preferred styles, such as modern minimalist, creamy, original wood, vintage, wabi-sabi, light luxury, or practical family storage.
- Reference brands or photos if available.
- Children, elderly family members, pets, allergies, pregnancy, urgent move-in, or formaldehyde sensitivity.
- Color/material preferences and whether the user accepts substitutes for lower budget or shorter timeline.

## Batch 5: Service preference

Ask:

- Need for local showroom visits or acceptance of remote consultation.
- Desired communication style: highly responsive, detailed proposal, strong designer taste, or low-intervention execution.
- Whether the user prefers a large brand, local factory/store, design studio, or independent custom team.
- Tolerance for longer lead time in exchange for better design or quality control.

## Follow-up triggers

Ask more when:

- Budget is described only as "not too expensive" or "reasonable."
- City is broad and service area may vary by district.
- The user says "whole house" but lists only wardrobes or only cabinets.
- The user's top priorities conflict, such as ultra-low price plus high design and urgent schedule.
- The table has multiple close candidates and one missing detail would decide the fit.
